The Best Sober Day Counter Apps in 2026

By the Sobermark team · Updated August 2026

A day counter sounds almost too simple to bother with. It is one number. But anyone who has quit anything knows what that number does: it turns an invisible effort into something you can look at, protect, and feel quietly proud of. On a rough evening, "day 47" is an argument all by itself.

Full disclosure: Sobermark is our app, so we are not neutral. We have tried to keep everything below fair, and we tell you when a rival is the better fit.

This guide is only about counters. Not programmes, not courses, not communities, just apps that count your days well and dress that number in enough context to keep it meaningful. Here are the five we would actually recommend.

Sober Time

If you want the most counter for your counter, Sober Time is hard to beat. It runs on iOS and Android, supports multiple addictions with a separate counter for each, and lets you customise the look of each one until it feels personal. It counts up in years, months, days, hours and even seconds if you like watching the number move.

There is a community forum attached, but the counters are the core. It is the tinkerer's choice: if you enjoy setting things up just so, you will be happy here. If you find options overwhelming, one of the simpler apps below may serve you better.

Sobermark

Sobermark is our app, so weigh this section accordingly. The counter is the centre of the app, and around it we show three numbers we think matter more than seconds ticking by: money saved, time reclaimed, and units avoided, all calculated from figures you enter about your own habit, not from averages.

You can count any habit, including alcohol, smoking, vaping, weed, gambling, or a custom one, and run them side by side. Milestones and badges mark your progress, progress cards let you share a milestone without sharing an app, and if a craving hits there are SOS tools built in: a hold-on breathing exercise and a ride-it-out timer. A slip resets the counter, but the app never scolds you for it, and Bern the sun bear keeps the tone kind. There is no account and no sign up; everything stays on your phone.

Core tracking is free, with Sobermark Pro at £14.99 a year after a 3 day free trial, £2.99 a week, or £24.99 lifetime. The catch: Android only for now. iPhone users will need Sober Time or I Am Sober instead.

I Am Sober

I Am Sober is more than a counter, but the counter at its heart is excellent: clear milestones, a satisfying progression, and a daily pledge ritual wrapped around it that turns checking the number into a habit of its own. Because it has a large community, every milestone comes with the voices of people who reached it before you, which gives the number a warmth a bare counter cannot manage.

It is on iOS and Android, with a free tier covering the essentials and a subscription on top; check the store listing for current details. Choose it if you want your counter to come with company. Skip it if you want your counter to come with silence.

Nomo

Nomo calls its counters clocks, and it lets you run as many as you like, free. Made by someone in recovery, it adds two thoughtful touches: small milestone chips you collect as the days add up, and accountability partners who can see your clocks. Sharing your counter with a sponsor or a trusted friend changes its character, because now someone else notices when it resets. If external accountability keeps you honest, that makes Nomo the best counter on this list for you. The interface is dated, but the clocks do their job.

Quitzilla

Quitzilla is an Android habit-quitting tracker that handles multiple counters with a couple of nice ideas of its own. The one we rate most is its reasons list: you write down why you are quitting each thing, and the app keeps those reasons next to the counter where you will actually see them. It also tracks money and time saved per habit. It is a general-purpose quitting tool rather than a sobriety app specifically, so it has no recovery flavour at all, which some people prefer and others find a little cold.

Who should pick what

On iPhone, your realistic picks here are Sober Time or I Am Sober, and I Am Sober is the better all-rounder of the two if you also want community. Want someone else to see your counter? Nomo. Want maximum customisation? Sober Time. Want a plain, no-philosophy habit counter on Android? Quitzilla.

Pick Sobermark if you are on Android and want a calm counter with meaningful numbers around it, private by default, with craving tools for the moments the number alone is not enough.

Common questions

Why does a day counter help at all?

It makes progress visible and loss concrete. Skipping a drink is invisible; breaking a 47 day streak is not. That asymmetry is exactly what makes a counter useful on a wobbly evening.

Should the counter reset after one slip?

Most apps reset, and we think that is honest. What matters is the tone around the reset. Your total days do not vanish from your history, and an app that shames you for day zero is an app you will delete.

Can I count more than one habit?

Yes. Sober Time, Nomo, Quitzilla and Sobermark all run multiple counters at once. I Am Sober is strongest when focused on one main habit.

Is the money saved figure accurate?

It is as accurate as what you tell it. Apps that ask for your own spend, like Sobermark and Quitzilla, will be closer to reality than ones using population averages. Treat it as a motivating estimate either way.
