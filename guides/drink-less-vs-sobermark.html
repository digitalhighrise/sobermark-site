Drink Less vs Sobermark: cutting down or stopping fully?

By the Sobermark team · Updated August 2026

This is an unusual comparison, because the two apps barely compete. Drink Less is a free app built by researchers at University College London to help people cut down their drinking, and it runs on iPhone. Sobermark is an Android app for stopping a habit completely, whether that is alcohol, smoking, vaping, weed, gambling or something you define yourself. Different goal, different platform, different philosophy.

Full disclosure: Sobermark is our app, so we are not neutral. We have tried to keep everything below fair, and we tell you when a rival is the better fit.

If you searched for this page, you are probably deciding between moderating and stopping, or you have switched phones and lost access to one of the two. Either way, the honest answer is that your goal and your handset will make this decision for you, and we will say plainly where Drink Less is the right call.

Two different goals: moderation and abstinence

Drink Less starts from the position that many people do not want to stop drinking entirely, they want to drink less, and that this is a legitimate and worthwhile goal. It comes out of a research group at UCL, it is free, and it is evidence-based in the proper sense: built on behaviour change research, not marketing language. Apps made by researchers are rare, and this is one of the good ones. Using it also supports ongoing research into what actually helps people change their drinking, which is a quietly generous reason to choose it.

Sobermark starts from the other position: you have decided a habit is leaving your life for good, and you want to watch the distance grow. There is no moderation mode and no drink diary, because the app is not trying to manage a habit, it is helping you leave one. The core of it is a day counter, and everything else, the stats, the journal, the craving tools, exists to keep that counter climbing.

Neither goal is superior. Plenty of people cut down successfully, and plenty of people find moderation harder than stopping. Only you, and ideally your doctor, can say which is right for you.

What each app gives you day to day

Drink Less is built around tracking what you drink and helping you steer it downward, using techniques drawn from behaviour change science. Its features are best described on its own listing, and because it is a research app, what you see may evolve as the evidence does. The headline is simple: it is free, credible and genuinely designed to help rather than to sell.

Sobermark counts the days since you stopped and turns your own numbers into money saved, time reclaimed and units avoided. There are milestones and badges as the weeks stack up, a journal with moods so you can spot your trigger patterns, and shareable progress cards for the milestones you want to celebrate out loud. For the hard moments there are craving SOS tools: a hold-on breathing exercise and a ride-it-out timer. Bern, a friendly sun bear, keeps you company throughout. If you slip, the counter resets and nobody scolds you. That is a firm design rule.

Platform, privacy and cost

Platform decides this for most people. Drink Less is an iPhone app. Sobermark is an Android app with no iOS version yet, and we would rather say that plainly than let you find out after reading a whole article. If you are on iOS and want to cut down, Drink Less is free and excellent value at that price. If you are on Android and want to stop, Drink Less simply is not an option.

On privacy, Sobermark asks for no account and no sign up, and your entries stay on your phone. There are no feeds or forums by design. Drink Less, as a research project, explains its own data practices clearly on its listing and in its studies, which is more transparency than most commercial apps offer.

On cost, Drink Less is free. Sobermark's core tracking is free too; Sobermark Pro is £14.99 a year with a 7 day free trial, £2.99 a week, or £24.99 once for lifetime access.

Who should pick what

Pick Drink Less if you have an iPhone and your goal is to drink less rather than not at all. It is free, made by people whose job is to study what works, and choosing it costs you nothing but the download. For iPhone moderation, it is the better fit than Sobermark by a wide margin, not least because Sobermark does not run on your phone.

Pick Sobermark if you are on Android and you have decided to stop fully, whether the habit is drink or something else entirely. A moderation app cannot celebrate day ninety with you, and a single-substance app cannot count your smoke-free days alongside your sober ones.

If you are on Android and want moderation rather than abstinence, look at Sunnyside or Try Dry instead. Sobermark is honestly not built for counting drinks, and pretending otherwise would waste your time.

Common questions

Is Drink Less available on Android?

It has been an iPhone app. Check its listing for any changes, but at the time of writing Android users need a different tool.

Can Sobermark help me moderate instead of stopping?

Not really. Sobermark counts days since you stopped, and its stats assume abstinence. Try Dry or Sunnyside suit moderation goals far better.

Which is better for quitting alcohol completely?

Between these two, Sobermark, because that is the job it is built for and Drink Less is aimed at cutting down. On iPhone, look at I Am Sober or Nomo instead.

Is Sobermark evidence-based like Drink Less?

We will not claim that. Drink Less comes from a university research group and has been studied properly. Sobermark is a well made tracker with craving tools, and we keep our claims to what it does: count, calculate and encourage.

No app is medical advice or a substitute for professional help. If stopping could affect your health, or things feel too heavy, speak to a doctor. Free, confidential helplines for every country are listed at findahelpline.com.
