Best gambling recovery apps in 2026

By the Sobermark team · Updated August 2026

Gambling is different from most habits people quit, because the thing you are quitting lives on the same phone you would use to track quitting it. That is why the honest answer to "what is the best gambling recovery app" is usually not one app but a small stack: something that blocks access, somewhere to get real support, and something that tracks the days as they build. This guide covers all three, and is written for the UK in particular, though most of it applies anywhere.

Full disclosure: Sobermark is our app, so we are not neutral. We have tried to keep everything below fair, and we tell you when a rival is the better fit. For gambling specifically, we would say plainly that a tracker like ours should not be the only tool you use.

Blocking tools: Gamban, BetBlocker and self exclusion

Blocking software is a different category from trackers, and for gambling it is the category to sort out first. Tools like Gamban and BetBlocker work by cutting off access to gambling sites and apps on your devices, so that a 2am urge meets a locked door instead of a login screen. In general terms, Gamban is a paid product that is often available free through support routes in the UK, and BetBlocker is a free charity run tool; check their own sites for current details.

In the UK you can also self exclude from online gambling through GAMSTOP, and from bookmakers and other premises through separate schemes. None of these are perfect, determined workarounds exist, but they add friction exactly where friction saves money and misery. If you do only one thing after reading this page, make it a blocker plus self exclusion.

Sobermark, our app, covers the tracking side. Gambling is one of the habits it supports directly: you set roughly what you were losing and how often you gambled, and it turns your gamble free time into days, money saved and time reclaimed, all from your own figures. With gambling the money saved number is often startling, and watching it climb is a genuinely different feeling from watching a balance fall.

GamCare and real support

GamCare is the UK charity behind the National Gambling Helpline, and its support is free, confidential and run by people who understand gambling harm specifically. That includes the helpline itself on 0808 8020 133, open 24 hours a day, plus online chat, forums and structured treatment routes. If money worries, debt or hiding things from people you love are part of the picture, this is the layer that actually addresses them.

No tracker or blocker replaces talking to someone. Plenty of people use the helpline once, early, just to say the problem out loud. That call costs nothing and commits you to nothing.

Quitzilla

Quitzilla, a general quitting tracker for Android, handles gambling the same way it handles any habit: a counter, a savings figure and a list of your own reasons for stopping. That reasons list earns its keep here, because gambling urges are excellent at arguing, and your own words from a clear headed moment argue back.

It has no gambling specific content or support links, and its design is functional rather than warm. But if you want several counters in one place, or just a plain tracker with no personality, it is a reasonable choice at the time of writing; check its store listing for what the paid tier covers.

Who should pick what

Everyone quitting gambling should start with a blocker and, in the UK, self exclusion through GAMSTOP. That comes before any tracker, ours included. If gambling has caused real financial or personal damage, make GamCare your second stop, not an app.

For tracking on Android, pick Sobermark if you want privacy, a calm tone, money saved from your real numbers and help in the moment an urge hits. Pick Quitzilla if you prefer something plainer or want gambling as one counter among several.

On iPhone, Sobermark is not available yet, so pair a blocker with a general sobriety counter that supports multiple addictions, such as Sober Time or I Am Sober, both of which also offer community support that some people find essential in gambling recovery.

Common questions

Can an app really stop me gambling?

A blocker can stop the impulsive 2am bet, which is worth a lot. But apps work at the level of access and habit, not the underlying pull. The people who do best tend to combine blocking, tracking and human support rather than relying on any one of them.

Why track days at all if the blocker does the work?

Because recovery needs something to grow, not just something to avoid. A day counter and a rising money saved figure give you visible proof that the harder choice is paying off, and that proof matters on bad days.

Is Sobermark private enough for this?

There is no account, no sign up and no server holding your history; entries stay on your phone. Nobody can stumble across a profile of yours because there are no profiles.

What if I place a bet after six weeks clean?

The counter resets and the app stays kind, because shame fuels relapse more than it prevents it. Tell someone you trust, tighten the blocking, and start the counter again the same day.

No app is medical advice or a substitute for professional help. If gambling is causing you harm, support is free and confidential: in the UK, call the National Gambling Helpline on 0808 8020 133 (GamCare), open 24 hours a day. If stopping could affect your health, or things feel too heavy, speak to a doctor. Free, confidential helplines for every country are listed at findahelpline.com.
