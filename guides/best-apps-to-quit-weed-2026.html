Best apps to quit weed in 2026

By the Sobermark team · Updated August 2026

Quitting weed gets less attention than quitting drinking or smoking, which can make it feel like you are the only one struggling with it. You are not. For a lot of people the hard parts are sleep, boredom and the evening ritual, not dramatic withdrawal, and progress is easy to lose track of because nothing about it is visible. That is exactly the gap a tracker fills: it gives you a number that grows, savings you can point at, and a record of the days you got through.

Full disclosure: Sobermark is our app, so we are not neutral. We have tried to keep everything below fair, and we tell you when a rival is the better fit.

There is no single famous quit weed app the way Smoke Free dominates cigarettes, so your real choice is between general trackers that handle weed well and smaller dedicated apps. Here is how we would choose in 2026.

Sobermark

Sobermark, our app, was built to track any habit you are quitting, and weed is one of the habits it supports directly. You set what you were spending and how often you used, and it turns your weed free time into money saved, time reclaimed and units avoided, all from your own numbers. Milestones and badges give the early weeks some shape, and a journal with moods lets you notice patterns, like which evenings are hardest.

Two things matter especially for weed. First, the craving SOS tools: a hold-on breathing exercise and a ride-it-out timer for the moment the evening pull kicks in. Second, privacy: there is no account, no sign up, and your entries never leave your phone, which matters when the thing you are quitting is not legal everywhere. Bern the sun bear keeps the tone warm, and a slip resets the counter without judgment. Core tracking is free; Pro is £14.99 a year with a 7 day free trial, £2.99 a week, or £24.99 lifetime. Android only for now, which is the main honest limitation.

Quitzilla

Quitzilla is a quitting tracker for Android that lets you set up counters for anything, weed included. Its best feature is simple: you write down your reasons for quitting, and the app keeps them in front of you. When you are three days in and telling yourself one joint will not matter, rereading your own words from a stronger moment genuinely helps.

It supports multiple counters, so weed can sit alongside alcohol or anything else you are cutting out. The interface is plain and there is no weed specific content, but as a free to start, flexible tracker it is a solid pick. Check the store listing for what sits behind its paid tier at the time of writing.

Dedicated quit weed apps

There is a small category of apps built only for cannabis, with Grounded being one of the better known names. In general terms, these apps combine a quit counter with weed specific content: withdrawal timelines, community spaces where people share how week two felt, and programme style guidance written for cannabis rather than adapted from smoking. For some people, seeing their exact experience described, the sleep disruption, the vivid dreams, the irritability, is worth more than any counter.

The trade offs are the usual ones for niche apps: small teams, uneven updates, and often a subscription for the full programme, so check current pricing and reviews on the store before committing. If you want to feel understood specifically as someone quitting weed, this category is worth exploring. If you mainly want a reliable counter, a general tracker does the same core job.

Generic habit trackers

Plenty of people quit weed with nothing more than a general habit app or even a streak counter, and it can work. If you already live in a habit tracker, adding a "no weed" streak costs nothing and keeps everything in one place.

The weakness is that generic tools treat quitting weed like drinking more water. There is no money saved calculation, no help in the craving moment, and no understanding that a slip is not the same as forgetting to stretch. If your relationship with weed is casual, a generic tracker may be enough. If quitting feels genuinely hard, a purpose built app earns its place on your home screen.

Who should pick what

If you want weed specific content and a community of people quitting the same thing, try a dedicated quit weed app like Grounded, and this is a genuine case where those apps are the better fit than Sobermark. The same goes for iPhone users: Sobermark is Android only for now, so look at dedicated apps or general trackers on iOS.

If you want your reasons for quitting front and centre and maybe several counters running at once, Quitzilla is a sensible, unfussy choice.

Pick Sobermark if privacy is high on your list, if you want your progress translated into money and time from your own numbers, and if you want something calm to reach for when a craving hits rather than a feed to scroll. And if you only need a nudge, not support, a generic habit tracker you already use beats installing anything new.

Common questions

Is weed withdrawal real?

Many regular users report disrupted sleep, vivid dreams, low mood and irritability for days or weeks after stopping. It is usually manageable, but if you are worried about how stopping might affect you, talk to a doctor rather than an app.

Why does privacy matter for a quit weed app?

Because cannabis is not legal everywhere, and a record of use tied to an account is something many people reasonably do not want. Sobermark keeps everything on your phone with no account at all; whatever app you choose, it is worth checking where your data goes.

How does Sobermark work out money saved?

You tell it roughly what you spent and how often, and it prices your weed free days from those figures. Watching that number pass the cost of something you actually want is a surprisingly strong motivator.

What if I have a smoke after two weeks off?

The counter resets, your badges and history stay, and nothing scolds you. Two weeks of changed habits does not vanish because of one evening.

No app is medical advice or a substitute for professional help. If stopping could affect your health, or things feel too heavy, speak to a doctor. Free, confidential helplines for every country are listed at findahelpline.com.
