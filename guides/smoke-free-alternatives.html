Smoke Free alternatives: 4 other apps for quitting smoking

By the Sobermark team · Updated August 2026

Smoke Free is one of the best known quit smoking apps, on iOS and Android, and its reputation is deserved. It is known for its missions, small daily tasks that give the first weeks some structure, and for health stats that show what quitting is doing for your body. If it is working for you, there is no reason to move.

People look for alternatives for ordinary reasons. Some do not want a daily task, they just want a counter. Some want to track vaping or other habits alongside cigarettes. Some want an app with no paid tier at all, or a different tone. All four options below answer at least one of those.

Full disclosure: Sobermark is our app, so we are not neutral. We have tried to keep everything below fair, and we tell you when a rival is the better fit.

1. EasyQuit

EasyQuit is the closest like for like swap on this list. It is an Android focused quit smoking tracker known for its health timeline, showing how your body is expected to recover milestone by milestone, and for a generous set of achievements that make progress feel rewarded.

If what you liked about Smoke Free was the health focused feedback but you want a different interface or structure, start here. Like Smoke Free it is built around cigarettes specifically, so it goes deep on smoking and nothing else. Check its store listing for current pricing, as free and paid features shift over time.

2. NHS Quit Smoking app

The NHS Quit Smoking app is the official free app from the NHS, built to support people through the first weeks of stopping smoking with daily encouragement and progress tracking. Being an official health service app, its advice is grounded in the same guidance you would get from NHS stop smoking support generally.

It is free, has no upsell, and pairs naturally with the rest of the NHS's stop smoking help, which is a genuinely strong combination if you are in the UK. It will not suit everyone: it is focused on the standard quit journey rather than long term tracking or multiple habits, and availability and features are best checked on the official NHS site or your app store. For a UK smoker who wants a trustworthy, free starting point, it is arguably the first thing to try.

3. Sobermark

Sobermark is a different shape of app: a calm, private tracker for any habit you are quitting, with smoking as one of its core options alongside vaping, alcohol, weed, gambling and custom habits. That makes it the pick if you are quitting cigarettes and something else at once, or if you expect to tackle vaping next and want it all in one place.

You get a day counter, money saved, time reclaimed and cigarettes avoided, all calculated from your own numbers, plus milestones, a journal with moods, and craving SOS tools: a hold on breathing exercise and a ride it out timer for the minutes when a craving peaks. Bern, a friendly sun bear, keeps you company, and a slip resets the counter without any scolding. There is no account or sign up; your data stays on your phone.

Being honest about the trade offs: it is Android only for now, and it does not have Smoke Free's missions or a smoking specific health timeline. Sobermark gives every new user a 3 day free trial, then costs £14.99 a year, £2.99 a week, or £24.99 once for lifetime.

4. Quitzilla

Quitzilla is a habit and addiction quitting tracker for Android. Like Sobermark it is not limited to smoking: it lets you run multiple counters at once, and it is known for its reasons lists, where you write down why you are quitting and the app reflects them back at you when resolve dips.

It sits closer to a straightforward counter than to a programme like Smoke Free. If the missions felt like homework and you mainly want to watch the days and the money saved stack up, with your own reasons on hand, Quitzilla does that simply and well. As ever, check the store listing for what is free and what is paid at the moment.

Who should pick what

In the UK and want free, official, no strings support: the NHS Quit Smoking app is the sensible first stop, honestly ahead of everything else here including Sobermark.

Want deep smoking specific feedback with an Android home: EasyQuit. On iPhone: Smoke Free itself remains one of your best options, since EasyQuit is Android focused and Sobermark has no iOS app yet.

Want a plain multi habit counter with your reasons front and centre: Quitzilla. Quitting smoking plus something else, and want a private, judgment free tracker with craving tools: that is Sobermark's territory.

Common questions

Is there a completely free alternative to Smoke Free?

The NHS Quit Smoking app is free. Sobermark gives every new user a 3 day free trial, then costs £14.99 a year, £2.99 a week, or £24.99 once for lifetime. For EasyQuit and Quitzilla, check their store listings for what currently sits behind a paywall.

Which app is best if I also vape?

Sobermark and Quitzilla can track vaping and smoking as separate habits at the same time. Smoke Free and EasyQuit are built around cigarettes.

Do these apps tell me how my health is recovering?

Smoke Free and EasyQuit are the ones known for health timelines. Sobermark and Quitzilla focus on days, money and your own motivations instead, and none of it is medical advice.

Can I keep my quit date if I switch apps?

Yes. Every tracker here lets you enter the date you quit, so your progress carries over rather than starting from zero.

No app is medical advice or a substitute for professional help. If stopping could affect your health, or things feel too heavy, speak to a doctor. Free, confidential helplines for every country are listed at findahelpline.com.
